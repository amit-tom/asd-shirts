The Suvin Standard

Less than 1kg, out of every 38 tons.

Suvin is a hybrid of Indian Sujata and Barbados Sea Island cotton, developed for one purpose: staple length and fineness no other commercial cotton reaches. This is why ASD builds every shirt from nothing else.

Grown exclusively in a handful of districts across Tamil Nadu and Karnataka, Suvin's extra-long staple fibres run finer and longer than Egyptian Giza — the benchmark most luxury shirting is measured against. At 2.9 micronaire, the fibre is thinner than a human hair split many times over, which is what gives finished cloth its characteristic lustre: light travels along a smoother, more continuous fibre surface instead of scattering across short, broken staples.

Why so rare

Suvin yields are naturally low and the plant is finicky to grow at scale, which is exactly why total global output stays under a few hundred tonnes a year against cotton's ~25 million tonne harvest. There is simply not enough Suvin fabric woven in a year to support mass production — a constraint ASD treats as a feature, not a limitation. We cap our commissions to what a single-needle bench can construct without compromising stitch density.

Why one fibre, one colour

We believe that after a certain point, choice becomes clutter. You no longer want variety; you demand excellence. That is why ASD dedicates its entire craft to a single material and a single colour: rare white Suvin cotton, engineered into ASD's proprietary 120/2 fabric — ends and picks constructed with the same precision as the fibre itself.

How it wears

Because the staple fibres are exceptionally long and resilient, the fabric softens with every wash while retaining its crisp weave structure and tensile strength — a shirt that improves with age rather than wearing out.
